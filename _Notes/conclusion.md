# Conclusion (whole report)

The conclusion is the last thing the reviewer reads — and the first thing they'll
flag if it's weak. It's also one of the most-neglected parts.

## What belongs

A conclusion has **exactly two parts**:

1. **Summary** — recap the report:
   - What contribution you made.
   - Which tools you used.
   - How you proceeded (methodology, key technical decisions).
   - Optionally, the difficulties you encountered (briefly).
2. **Perspectives** — forward-looking:
   - Features that could be added to your application.
   - Directions for future improvement.
   - Open questions or limits you didn't have time to address.

That's it. Two parts. Both short.

## What does **not** belong

> This is one of the most important and yet most neglected parts of the report.
> What you should **not** do here is talk about how rewarding the internship was,
> how it taught you to integrate into a team, what the working world is like,
> and so on.
>
> Frankly, nobody cares — at least not in this section. That kind of material
> belongs in the Acknowledgements or in the Dedication.

So, banned from the conclusion:

- Internship experience commentary ("I learned a lot…", "great team…").
- Personal growth ("I discovered a passion for…").
- Vague hand-waves ("this project has many possibilities").
- New content not mentioned anywhere else in the report. The conclusion is
  *closing*, not *introducing*.

## Format

- **No numbered sections** inside the conclusion — write it as one or two
  paragraphs per part.
- The chapter title is **"Conclusion and Perspectives"** (or "Conclusion générale"
  in French versions). The template wires this up in `\backmatter`.
- Don't put a per-chapter Introduction / Conclusion inside the conclusion chapter
  itself — that would be recursive nonsense.

## Length

Roughly **1 to 2 pages**. Shorter than a body chapter, longer than the
acknowledgements. If yours is half a page, you're not saying enough about
perspectives. If it's 5 pages, you've started telling the story again.

## Writing order

Counter-intuitive but true: **write the introduction and the conclusion last**,
after the body chapters are done. The body shapes what you can credibly claim
to have done and what perspectives are honest.
