\section*{Introduction}
A theoretical study \cite{YOUSFI2015} typically contains one or both of the
following parts.

\section{State of the Art}
This is a detailed survey of what already exists on the market or in the
literature (hence the name ``state of the art''). The goal is to compare
existing solutions, analyse the result of that comparison, and explain why
none of them fully address the problem at hand.
\begin{figure}[!ht]\centering
  \includegraphics[scale=0.9]{art.jpg}
  \caption{State of the art}
  \label{fig:fig1}
\end{figure}

\section{Study of the Existing System}
This is usually performed when extending an existing piece of software or
modifying an existing application. The study describes what is already in
place in your working environment.

\section*{Conclusion}
The conclusion is usually unnumbered and does not appear in the table of
contents.
